# NO SUS. — Eureka presentation practice script

Target runtime: 5–7 minutes. The words below are a rehearsal spine, not a demand to speak quickly. Let the black space and the questions breathe.

## Before the presentation starts

### Ten minutes before

- Ask the judges for permission before handling their phones. Say: “Before we start, may I open two links on your phones so you can inspect the live product yourself?”
- On one phone, open the NO SUS Android app and sign in. Leave the Workspace screen visible.
- On the other phone, open `https://app.nosus.foo/` in a browser and leave the recipient/browser-ready page available. Keep `https://nosus.foo/` ready as the public landing page if they want to see it.
- Check Wi-Fi, brightness, sound off, notification banners, and that the deck browser is in fullscreen. Do not ask the room to wait while you troubleshoot.
- Have one real document ready for the live share flow. Do not use a judge’s personal document.
- Keep the deck and live product in separate browser tabs. The deck is the story; the app is the proof.
- Start the local presentation server and open the printed `Phone:` URL on your phone. Tap `NEXT`, `PREV`, and one direct scene number before the judges arrive. Keep the phone unlocked and in a discreet presentation grip.

### Just before slide 01

Say, while the phones are being handed back or the judges are looking at the prepared screens:

> “I’ve left the NO SUS app and web app open so you can try the product in your own hands at the end. I’ll start with the question the product is built around.”

Then start the deck. Do not explain the product yet.

During the film, use the phone’s `NEXT` button to advance. Use `PREV` only to recover from an accidental advance; use the scene grid during rehearsal, not in front of the judges.

## The film

### 01 — The question (about 15 seconds)

**On screen:** `WHAT HAPPENS / AFTER YOU / PRESS SEND?`

Say nothing for the first two beats. Then:

> “We all know how to press Send. The part we usually don’t know is what happens next.”

Hold on the question. Let the small `NO SUS.` in the corner arrive without pointing it out.

### 02 — The send (about 20 seconds)

As `CONFIDENTIAL.pdf` crosses the screen, say:

> “You can send a sensitive document in seconds.”

When the file disappears and the question mark remains:

> “But what do you know after you send it?”

Pause. Do not rush to solve the tension.

### 03 — The gap (about 15 seconds)

Let each question replace the previous one. Say them with the screen, one at a time:

> “Who opened it?”
>
> “When?”
>
> “The right person?”
>
> “What happened next?”

Then:

> “That gap is where sensitive information becomes anonymous.”

### 04 — Not another Drive (about 15 seconds)

When `STORAGE IS SOLVED.` appears:

> “Storage is solved. Store, share, collaborate — those workflows already work.”

As the final line arrives:

> “NO SUS is not trying to replace storage. We are solving the layer after sharing.”

### 05 — The reveal (about 15 seconds)

Let the mark appear first. Then say:

> “NO SUS is the layer between Send and Leak.”

Do not define every feature here. This is the reveal; the next scene earns the explanation.

### 06 — The product (about 45–60 seconds)

This is the first live-product scene. Follow the phone and use these lines as the on-screen state changes:

1. **UPLOAD** — “A document enters the workspace.”
2. **CREATE TRACKED LINK** — “The share creates a tracked link that still reports home: `app.nosus.foo/#/r/`.”
3. **IDENTIFY** — “The recipient is not anonymous. Here, the recipient resolves as SCHOLAR.”
4. **VIEW** — “They open it in the secure viewer. The page is watermarked while it is being viewed.”
5. **TRACK** — “And the sender sees the access event: Document Viewed.”

Close the scene with the product claim:

> “NO SUS lets you know who accessed a shared sensitive document, and what happened after sharing.”

Do not say that this prevents screenshots, that the server cannot see the file, or that the future AI architecture already exists. The honest claim is identity-aware sharing, a watermarked viewer, and document activity.

### 07 — The loop (about 15 seconds)

Read the first line left to right:

> “Upload. Share. Identify. View. Track.”

Then point to the contrast:

> “The old mental model is Send and Hope. The NO SUS model is Send, Identify, View, Track.”

### 08 — The belief (about 10 seconds)

Read the statement slowly:

> “A file should not become anonymous the moment it is shared.”

Hold. This is the product philosophy, not another feature list.

### 09 — Proof: it’s live (about 30 seconds)

When the three real screens arrive:

> “This is not a concept. It’s live.”

Point to the workspace, viewer, and audit screen:

> “Working Android app. Working web app. Tracked sharing. Recipient browser flow.”

Then make the credibility transition explicit:

> “The product exists today. Now I want to show you the larger direction it makes possible.”

At this point, the judges should believe the current product before any future architecture appears.

## The direction (slides 10–16b)

Say this once before slide 10:

> “Everything from here is direction — future architecture, not a current product claim.”

### 10 — Document Passport (about 20 seconds)

> “That made us ask a bigger question: what if a document had an identity?”

As the history builds:

> “Created, modified, shared, viewed, downloaded, transformed — a persistent provenance record.”

Then:

> “Today is the first accountability layer. The rest is the direction.”

### 11 — After AI (about 15 seconds)

> “And then AI arrived. A document is no longer accessed only by a person. It can be touched by a human, an application, or an AI agent.”

When the two questions appear:

> “Who is allowed to read it? And who is allowed to act on it?”

### 12 — Not enough (about 15 seconds)

Start with the quiet line:

> “We know who opened your document.”

Pause. Then:

> “But knowing who opened a document isn’t enough anymore. What happens when the thing opening it isn’t a person?”

### 13 — The future USP (about 15 seconds)

Read the kicker so the boundary is unmistakable:

> “NO SUS Next. Future architecture.”

Then deliver the memorable line and hold:

> “Don’t give AI your document. Give it a capability.”

### 14 — Private AI concept (about 30 seconds)

Say before the mock policy card:

> “This is a concept, not a shipped feature. Imagine authorizing one narrow operation for one agent.”

As the policy appears:

> “The agent may check for PII and answer predefined questions. It cannot download, export, or keep unrestricted access.”

As the result and audit stamps appear:

> “The result comes back, the raw document is not disclosed, and the permission expires. The audit records the operation.”

Close:

> “The AI gets permission for one operation — not ownership of the file.”

### 15 — Access lifecycle (about 20 seconds)

> “The passport now has to hold more than a person. Not only who opened it — what was allowed.”

Read the lifecycle quickly but clearly:

> “Created. Shared. Human accessed. AI agent authorized. Operation executed. Result generated. Permission revoked.”

Tie it back to the present:

> “That future record extends the audit log you saw a moment ago.”

### 16 — Architecture (about 20 seconds)

> “The enabling technologies could include confidential computing, MPC, zero-knowledge proofs, Midnight, and privacy-preserving AI.”

Then immediately ground it:

> “But NO SUS is not selling a bag of cryptography. NO SUS is the product layer: policy, identity, and audit around a verified result.”

### 16b — Positioning (about 20 seconds)

Read the three rows:

> “Traditional file sharing is Send and Hope. Existing AI workflows upload and the AI gets the data. NO SUS is Share, Control, Process, Verify.”

Then:

> “We are not more encrypted. NO SUS gives information permissions, not just links.”

## Business and founder (slides 17–20)

### 17 — Market (about 20 seconds)

> “We start narrow and expand naturally.”

Point across the sectors:

> “Universities first, then research, professional services, and business.”

Then the model:

> “The path is Free, Pro, Team, Organization.”

Do not quote a market-size number. The deck intentionally uses no invented statistics.

### 18 — The difference (about 20 seconds)

> “Traditional file sharing gives you storage, collaboration, and access.”

Then point to NO SUS:

> “NO SUS adds identity-aware sharing, watermarked viewing, document activity, and controlled external access.”

Close:

> “We are not replacing storage. We are building the accountability layer around sharing.”

### 20 — Founder (about 35 seconds)

Start with the facts on screen:

> “I’m Shubham Sahu, founder — product and engineering. I built this in 15 days, solo.”

As the pipeline appears:

> “Idea, design, code, backend, Android, web, deployment.”

Then the honest builder insight:

> “AI made building software faster. It didn’t make building the right thing easy.”

Use the ask as an invitation, not a plea:

> “I’m looking for the people who can help take this from something I built alone into something people actually need — a co-founder, an early team, strategic partners, and capital.”

If time is tight, cut the paragraph about software becoming easier to build; keep the sentence about execution, product judgment, distribution, and persistence as your final founder line.

## Close and judge action (slide 21)

### Beat A

> “Today, NO SUS tells you who accessed your information.”

Pause.

### Beat B

> “Tomorrow, NO SUS can decide what a human, application, or AI agent is allowed to do.”

Pause.

### Beat C

> “The question isn’t only: who has my data? What are they allowed to do with it?”

Let the mark and QR arrive. Then say:

> “The QR is there because I want you to inspect it, not take my word for it. Scan it, open the product, and try the flow yourselves.”

Finish with:

> “NO SUS isn’t just trying to protect a file. It’s trying to make information accountable after it leaves your hands.”

Hold on `NO SUS.`. Do not add “thank you” over the final mark; let the image finish.

## If the live product or network fails

Do not apologize for more than one sentence. Say:

> “The live screen is unavailable at this moment, so I’ll use the captured product state as evidence and keep the claim narrow: the Android and web flows are built, and the current product is the identity-aware sharing, watermarked viewing, and audit path you just saw.”

Then continue the deck. The judges already have the prepared app/web tabs and can inspect them after the presentation.

## Practice checklist

- Rehearse the first 45 seconds until it feels conversational, not memorized.
- Practice the slide 06 product lines while looking at the screen, not at the judges’ phones.
- Time the full run once at normal pace and once with longer holds; both should stay between 5 and 7 minutes.
- The words “future architecture,” “concept,” and “direction” must be spoken before the AI mockup.
- Never claim current AI permissions, Document Passport provenance, zero-knowledge implementation, screenshot-proofing, user counts, revenue, or partnerships unless you can show them live and verify them.
- End with the question, the QR, and the invitation to test.
